## Moves

- [HIGH] (2026-07-03) The size of the training apparatus built to help insiders sell/explain a thing IS a measurement of that thing's explanation cost — read the apparatus as evidence, not as tooling. Anything with high explanation cost cannot be sold by description to buyers who've never bought its category (they have no shelf for it); it can only be sold by demonstration, or by slotting into a shelf they already own. Corollary: the audience the apparatus was written for is often the natural buyer being ignored.
- [HIGH] (2026-06-28) When someone wants outcome Y from the asset they're proudest of, don't ask whether the asset is big/good enough — ask whether its *structure* is the opposite of what Y requires. A thing that maximizes dependence on its creator cannot produce an outcome that requires independence from the creator. Magnitude is a decoy; direction is the test. The asset they're most proud of can be the precise cause of the outcome they can't reach.
- [HIGH] (2026-06-28) A signal spreads on the *carrier's* incentive to carry it, not the *originator's* correctness. To predict whether a thing travels, score what the repeater gains by repeating it — never whether it's true or complete. Completeness serves the author's status; repeatability serves the messenger's. Optimizing "is this right/whole?" when the goal is spread is optimizing the wrong party's payoff.
- [HIGH] (2026-06-28) Spread and tether are different axes. Maximal transmissibility can defeat the goal when the goal requires attribution — the item that spreads most easily often carries its parent least, and past a point "more spreadable" becomes "more orphaning." If the aim is to make the *source* known (not just the item), measure tether, not reach. A thing can win by traveling so well it severs from whoever launched it.
- [HIGH] (2026-06-28) An opponent made of a *belief* dies at consensus — once everyone agrees, the fight is over and any identity built on it collapses. An opponent made of a *behavior/reflex* survives agreement: people concede the point and keep doing the thing anyway (the relapse). Durability comes from picking a relapse to fight, not a position to refute. Test a chosen adversary: can it be killed by agreement? If yes, it's a burnout villain.
- [HIGH] (2026-06-28) When everyone evaluates a thing on its obvious axis (will it last? is it big?), the binding constraint is often on an axis no one is measuring (is it already crowded? is it ownable?). Before optimizing the headline property, confirm it's the one that's actually scarce. A thing can score maximally on its obvious axis and still be useless because the real shortage lives orthogonal to where everyone is looking.
- [MED] (2026-06-28) The complete/systematic version of a thing is often the *output* of adoption, not its input — and building it first can be avoidance wearing the costume of preparation. When impressive groundwork precedes the outcome it's "for," check: is this the work, or a sophisticated place to hide from the smaller, scarier act that actually triggers the outcome? Followers write the scripture after the prophet; the prophet didn't earn followers by writing it first.
- [MED] (2026-06-28) A set of similar-looking items (three lines, three options, three assets) may be functionally different machines — one recruits, one credentials, one spreads. Deploying them as equals lets the highest-spread one outrun and cannibalize the others. Classify by *job* before treating them as interchangeable; surface resemblance hides divergent function.
- [HIGH] (2026-06-27) When a source credits an outcome to luck / accident / randomness, split accidental-WHICH from accidental-WHETHER: a selector you don't control picks among inventory you *do* control. The leverage sits upstream (number of attempts × fitness of the base they sit on × time spent surviving); the selection itself is not your business. "Embrace randomness" is fatalism that hides the controllable levers in a different place than the eye is pointed — find them.
- [HIGH] (2026-06-27) When the advice-giver IS the survivor narrating their own success, treat the narrator as a biased instrument (Move 2 applied to a person): survivorship + socially-rewarded humility manufacture "it was all accidental." The identical actor who lost narrates the identical behavior as "the game is rigged." Same actions, opposite story, decided by an outcome neither controlled — so extract only the part the uncontrolled outcome can't reach.
- [MED] (2026-06-27) When something is praised for "breaking the rules / being illogical," check whether it actually *parasitizes* the convention rather than escaping it: it reads as a break only to an audience that holds the rule, so the convention is ammunition, not obstacle. "Be unconventional" really means "violate the audience's single strongest unspoken expectation" — and it rides on top of an already-trusted base; strip the base and the clever move multiplies zero.
- [MED] (2026-06-27) A confessed chronic difficulty ("we just can't stop X in time") can be the symptom of straining to occupy a role the system doesn't grant the actor — playing *selector* in a game where you're only the *producer*. Locate which role the actor actually controls before treating the difficulty as a willpower/discipline problem.
- [HIGH] (2026-06-27) A step in your own method that prescribes the *shape* of the answer ("find where X stalled," "name the behavior") is a biased instrument hiding as a neutral move — run Instrument's Bias on your own checklist. Clean moves only ask a question or strip away vocabulary; they never arrive carrying a conclusion-class. Test: would the move still make sense in a field where that answer-shape doesn't exist?
- [HIGH] (2026-06-27) Read the literal words of the ask; the obvious frame often substitutes an easier near-synonym question for the one posed. Name the swap before answering.
- [HIGH] (2026-06-27) Ask what answer the instrument/dataset/report is structurally built to produce, then ask what you'd see without it. The tool shapes the diagnosis.
